ImproveMyself: An AI Logistics Assistant for Everyday Life

“People don't struggle because they don't know what to do. They struggle because there are hundreds of tiny logistics that occupy mental space.” An early-stage concept for an AI assistant that unifies to-do lists, habit tracking, notes, and errand routing into one low-friction system.

Overview

Most productivity apps pick one lane: Todoist for tasks, Streaks for habits, Notion for notes, Google Maps for navigation. ImproveMyself starts from a different premise — real life doesn't compartmentalize that way, so the assistant shouldn't either. The core idea is a single low-friction "Brain Dump" input, typed or spoken, that an AI layer parses into tasks, shopping lists, habit triggers, and optimized errand routes, instead of asking the user to sort and categorize everything themselves.

The Problem

People don't fail at self-improvement because they don't know what to do — they fail because hundreds of small logistics (pick up dry cleaning, remember a meeting printout, buy eggs, fit in a run) occupy mental space that should be going toward the actual goal. Existing tools force users to manually sort each of these into a separate app and manually plan around them, which reintroduces the exact cognitive overhead the tools are supposed to remove. The working thesis: the app shouldn't just organize tasks — it should organize intentions.

My Process

1

Concept framing

Defined the core thesis driving every later decision: the app shouldn't just organize tasks, it should organize intentions — reducing the mental space logistics occupy, rather than just relocating that overhead into a to-do list.

2

Feature ideation

Mapped four foundational mechanics: a frictionless "Brain Dump" voice/text input that lets someone say "buy eggs, drop off dry cleaning by 5, finish my math outline" and have the AI sort it; context-aware reminders triggered by location or situation instead of static time alerts; a Micro-Habit Engine that suggests small opportunistic actions based on real gaps in the day's schedule; and a "Smart Route" errand optimizer that sequences physical-world tasks geographically.

3

Differentiation research

Positioned the concept against single-purpose competitors (task managers, habit trackers, note apps, navigation tools) to identify what an AI "glue" layer across all four would need to do differently — and surfaced a real gap: no major app treats errands, habits, and daily logistics as one unified physical-world problem.

4

Open research questions

Scoped what still needs investigation before design starts: current app usage patterns, user journey mapping, pain points, and specific user needs — plus a first flagged risk, data privacy, since the assistant would need location, calendar, and goal data to function.

Takeaways

💡 Reframing the problem changed the whole feature set

Once the working problem statement became “organize intentions, not tasks,” static time-based reminders and single-purpose to-do lists stopped being sufficient answers — the concept needed context-awareness and cross-domain integration instead.

🔗 The best feature is often the connective one

Task managers, habit trackers, and maps already exist individually. The differentiated value is in the AI layer that connects them — like the Smart Route errand optimizer — not in reinventing any single piece on its own.
