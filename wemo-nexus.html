Building a Bilingual Digital Front Door for a B2B Scooter Fleet

Designed and launched WeMoNexus, the enterprise-facing site that turned a scooter-fleet product into a credible pitch for global B2B buyers — and a 20% demo-to-pilot conversion rate.

Overview

WeMo's B2B scooter-fleet product was strong, but its digital presence wasn't built for the buyers evaluating it: enterprise stakeholders comparing fleet partners across markets, in two languages, under real time pressure. I owned the design and build of WeMoNexus (still live at wemonexus.com ↗) end to end — from information architecture to the go-to-market motion that used it. The site positions Nexus as a single integrated platform spanning Fleet, Operations, and Customer management, built on WeMo Scooter's five years of operating a 7,000+ vehicle fleet.

The Problem

Enterprise buyers don't make fleet-partner decisions off a spec sheet. They need to see that a vendor can scale, support them locally, and speak their language — literally and organizationally. WeMo didn't have a site that did any of that; it had a strong operational story (30K+ daily rentals, 5+ years running a shared-mobility fleet) but no consistent way to translate it into a credible pitch for a bilingual, enterprise audience.

My Process

1

Discover

Audited existing sales collateral and pulled objections directly from the sales team's demo notes to understand what was actually stalling pilot decisions. WeMo's actual differentiator — 5+ years of operational experience running 7,000+ vehicles in the field — wasn't showing up anywhere in the pitch.

2

Decide

Defined the sitemap, content hierarchy, and component reuse patterns needed to translate B2B scooter-fleet features into an intuitive, bilingual (English/Mandarin) client journey. Split the site into a clear "Manage" arc — Operations, Hardware/Software, Growing your customer base — so different buyer roles could self-select into the section relevant to them.

3

Design & Build

Designed and launched WeMoNexus (still live at wemonexus.com) in WordPress: a dashboard-style UI section, a Fleet/Operations/Customer value framing, and proof points like the Sentinel Black Box data module and WeMo Life expansion, positioned to signal platform maturity rather than a single-feature tool. Produced client-facing pitch decks with hi-fidelity layouts and visual hierarchy aligned to enterprise stakeholder mental models.

4

Go-to-Market

Built and managed a 250+ ICP prospect pipeline in Apollo, ran 30+ tailored product demos, and iterated the site and decks based on cross-functional product and sales feedback.

Takeaways

🗺️ IA decisions are sales decisions

The sitemap and content hierarchy directly shaped which objections came up in demos — getting the structure right upstream made the sales conversation easier downstream.

🏗️ Operational proof beats feature lists

Enterprise buyers didn't need another features page — they needed evidence WeMo had already run this at scale. Leading with the 5-year, 7,000+ vehicle operating history did more to build credibility than any spec sheet.
