Overview

RISE started as a simple question: why do so many people quit the gym within a few months? Our team's hypothesis was that motivation isn't the real problem — consistency is, and consistency is easier with a partner. I led the design from early research through a validated hi-fi prototype, defining the partner-matching flow, the component system, and the interaction patterns the rest of the team built on.

The Problem

Existing fitness-partner apps treat matching like dating apps: swipe on a profile, message a stranger, hope it works out. But gym-goers we interviewed weren't worried about finding a partner — they were worried about meeting a stranger they couldn't verify was reliable, safe, or actually going to show up. The stated problem ("help people find workout partners") wasn't the real problem ("help people trust a stranger enough to meet them at a gym").

My Process

1

Discover

Ran user interviews and a competitive analysis of existing fitness and workout-matching apps. Synthesized findings into affinity maps, personas, and journey maps to find where trust broke down in the existing options.

2

Define

The affinity mapping surfaced a consistent pattern: users didn't want more matching options, they wanted accountability and safety signals before agreeing to meet someone. This became the core design insight that shaped every decision after.

3

Design

Moved from low-fidelity wireframes to interactive hi-fidelity prototypes in Figma, covering onboarding, goal/schedule filtering, and the matching flow. Defined a component hierarchy and reusable UI patterns so the team could scale the design system past the first flow, applying Nielsen's heuristics across every interaction decision.

4

Validate

Delivered annotated redlines and interaction specs for engineering handoff, and facilitated design critique sessions with the cross-functional team to pressure-test the flow before it was considered final.

Takeaways

🔒 Trust is a design material

Safety and accountability signals mattered as much as the matching logic itself. The best-designed matching algorithm doesn't help if people don't feel safe acting on it.

🧩 Component systems save critique time

Defining reusable patterns early meant design reviews could focus on flow and logic instead of re-litigating spacing and button styles every session.
