Accessibility Audit

Making a Research Lab's Website Usable for Everyone

A WCAG 2.1 AA audit of a research lab's public website, surfacing and prioritizing 10+ violations that automated scans alone would have missed.

Overview

The lab's public website is often the first thing prospective students, collaborators, and study participants interact with — and for users with visual or motor impairments, it wasn't working. I audited four core pages against WCAG 2.1 AA, combining automated scanning with manual testing to find the barriers that actually block task completion, then delivered a roadmap the team could execute without a full redesign.

The Problem

A quick automated scan can catch missing alt text or a contrast ratio violation, but it can't tell you whether a keyboard-only user can actually complete a task, or whether a screen reader announces a form correctly. The lab needed to know which issues were cosmetic and which were genuinely exclusionary — and needed that answer prioritized, not just itemized.

My Process

1

Automated Scan

Ran WAVE and aXe across four core pages to catch surface-level violations quickly: missing alt text, insufficient color contrast, and structural markup issues.

2

Manual Testing

Tested keyboard-only navigation and screen reader flows across six user groups to surface the barriers automated tools miss — focus traps, broken heading hierarchy, and inadequate ARIA labeling.

3

Prioritize

Ranked the 10+ violations found by severity and real user impact — distinguishing issues that fully blocked task completion from ones that degraded but didn't prevent it.

4

Recommend

Delivered a prioritized remediation roadmap targeting structural information architecture and fixes the team could implement without a full site rebuild.

Takeaways

🔍 Automated tools catch the surface

WAVE and aXe found the easy issues fast, but manual keyboard and screen-reader testing found the barriers that actually stopped someone from completing a task.

🎯 Prioritization is a design skill

Ranking fixes by user impact, not just violation count, is what made the roadmap something the team could realistically act on.
